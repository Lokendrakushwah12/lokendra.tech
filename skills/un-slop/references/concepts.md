# Finding a concept

A concept is a concrete thing from the app's own world that the interface can
be built out of. It replaces the question "what should this look like?", which
has no answer, with "what would this look like if it were made of *that*?",
which has many.

## Where to look

Work through these for the specific app. Each is a source of candidates, and
the good ones tend to come from the later sources because the first is where
everyone looks.

1. **The subject's physical world.** The objects, places and surfaces of the
   activity. Running: lane markings, a stopwatch, race bibs, a track's curve.
   Cooking: a recipe card, a chalkboard, tiles, a scale.
2. **The printed matter around it.** Tickets, labels, manuals, maps, score
   sheets, ledgers, field guides, timetables, packaging. Print has strong,
   specific typography and layout conventions that screens rarely borrow.
3. **The moment of use.** When and where the app is opened. Half asleep in the
   dark, one-handed on a train, sweating and out of breath, at a counter with a
   queue behind. The moment suggests light, density and pace.
4. **A place or culture tied to the subject.** A language course and the
   country's signage, playing cards or packaging. A coffee app and the
   roastery's sacks and stamps. Use this with care and specificity; a real
   artefact, not a national cliche.
5. **A material or process.** Paper cut-outs, letterpress, risograph, enamel
   pins, blueprint, woven fabric, ceramic glaze. A material brings its own
   rules for edges, colour and texture.
6. **The data's own shape.** What the app's information naturally looks like: a
   week is a strip, a route is a line, a budget is a pile, a streak is a chain.
   Sometimes the layout concept is simply the data drawn honestly.

Write at least five candidates. Take them from at least three different
sources.

## What a concept must decide

For each candidate, fill in one line per row. A candidate with blank rows is a
theme and should be dropped.

| | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Typography** | Which kind of type belongs to this thing: a condensed sans from signage, a serif from a field guide, numerals from a scoreboard? |
| **Layout** | What is the screen's structure borrowed from: lanes, a ticket, a ledger, a shelf, a map? |
| **Colour** | Where does the palette come from, and how few colours does the real thing use? |
| **Illustration or material** | What are things drawn or made of: cut paper, ink, engraved line, photography of real objects? |
| **Motion** | How does this thing move in the real world, and what is the one transition that shows it? |

### Worked example: a running app, "athletics track"

| | |
|---|---|
| Typography | Heavy condensed numerals, as on a race bib and a lap clock |
| Layout | Horizontal lanes; each run is a lane, the week is the track's straight |
| Colour | Track red, lane-line white, infield green; nothing else |
| Material | Flat paint with slightly rough edges, like painted lane lines |
| Motion | Progress runs along the lane left to right; a finished run crosses a line |

Every row is filled and every row is specific. The home screen can be sketched
from the table alone, which is the test.

### The same app, a weak candidate: "energetic and bold"

| | |
|---|---|
| Typography | A bold sans |
| Layout | (nothing) |
| Colour | Orange gradient |
| Material | (nothing) |
| Motion | "Snappy" |

It decides a colour and a weight. Everything else will be filled in by habit,
which means cards, a gradient header and a tab bar.

## Tests

- **Sketch test.** Can you describe the home screen's layout from the concept
  sentence alone, before designing it? If not, the concept is not doing layout.
- **Swap test.** Would the concept work unchanged for an app in a different
  category? "Dark with neon accents" fits a bank, a game and a gym, so it is
  not a concept for any of them.
- **Noun test.** Is the concept a noun you could photograph? "Calm" fails;
  "a village at dusk" passes.
- **Five-screen test.** Does it still have something to say on the settings
  screen and the empty state, or only on the hero? A concept that runs out
  after one screen needs a quieter mode, decided now.
- **Usability test.** Name the place where it will fight legibility or touch
  targets, and decide in advance that usability wins there.

## Making three directions genuinely different

Pick the three candidates that differ most in **layout** and **typography**,
because those two decide how a screen reads at a glance. Colour differences
alone do not make a direction.

A quick check: describe each direction in one sentence without naming a
colour. If two sentences are the same, merge them and promote a fourth
candidate.

## When the user already has a brand

Keep the fixed elements and find the concept around them. A logo and one colour
still leave typography, layout, material and motion open. Say what is fixed and
what the concept decides.

## Restraint

The concept sets the frame; the content stays plain. A reader should feel it in
the structure of the screen, in the numerals, in one transition, and not have
to fight it to read a list. If every button is a themed object, the app has
become a costume.
